## 🧠 Core Concepts

Three ideas from today's pages, which are the reason this book is in the plan:

**1. The black box argument.** Nield's case is that most people using machine learning cannot explain why their model produced its output — and that this is dangerous when models make decisions about people. Whatever you build in the next eighty days, you should be able to open it.

**2. This is not a maths crash course.** He says so himself. Chapter 1 covers only what Chapters 2–7 need, and it does it with Python rather than pencil and paper. That is why it fits in a plan like this at all.

**3. Functions, in the mathematical sense.** A function maps inputs to outputs. You already write Python functions. Chapter 1 is about the mathematical kind — `f(x) = 2x + 1` — because by Chapter 5, "fitting a model" means *finding the function that best maps your inputs to your outputs*. The word means the same thing in both places. Notice that now and Day 71 gets easier.

### The one connection worth holding

You are enrolled in **MAT 265 / MAT 170 (Calculus)** this semester. Chapter 1 of this book covers limits, derivatives, the chain rule and integrals — the same syllabus, taught in about forty pages with Python instead of a semester of problem sets.

This is the only topic in the whole ninety days where **one study hour counts twice.** That is why Chapter 1 is scheduled across Days 10, 18, 19 and 25 rather than left for Month 2 — your calculus course is happening *now*, and the two should be studied together.
