## 🎯 The Mission

By **14 December 2026** you should be able to do six things you cannot do today:

| # | Capability | How it gets proven |
|:-:|:-----------|:-------------------|
| 1 | Write Python — including object-oriented Python — without fighting the language | Closed-book assessments on Days 26 and 90; a working OOP toolkit you wrote |
| 2 | Take a messy real dataset and turn it into defensible findings | Project 1 (Pakistan inflation), shipped Day 45 |
| 3 | Answer business questions in SQL, from joins to window functions | Project 2 (retail analytics), shipped Day 64 |
| 4 | Explain *why* a statistic or model works, not just call `.fit()` | Essential Math Ch. 1–6 completed with code |
| 5 | Build, validate and honestly critique a machine learning model | Project 3, shipped Day 85 |
| 6 | Show three portfolio repositories a stranger can run and understand | Portfolio pass, Days 88–90 |

**What this is explicitly not:** a certificate run, a video marathon, a claim of seniority, or a job-application sprint. You are a first-semester undergraduate. The output of these 90 days is *capability and evidence*, which is what makes 2027 internship applications real.

## 🗺️ The Learning Sequence

```text
        PYTHON IDIOM                    ← Days 1–14   (you can already program;
             ↓                                          this is a translation layer)
        OOP, PROPERLY                   ← Days 15–26  (your weakest area, four teachers,
             ↓                                          also a head start on CSE 205)
        NUMPY → PANDAS                  ← Days 29–37  (arrays are the substrate,
             ↓                                          dataframes are the job)
        CLEANING → EDA → VISUALS        ← Days 38–44
             ↓
        ▣ PROJECT 1  Pakistan inflation ← ships Day 45
             ↓
        PROBABILITY → STATISTICS        ← Days 46–56  (from your own book, with code)
             ↓
        SQL ───────────────────────────── runs in parallel from Day 33 onward
             ↓
        BUSINESS METRICS                ← Day 57
             ↓
        ▣ PROJECT 2  Retail analytics   ← ships Day 64
             ↓
        LINEAR ALGEBRA                  ← Days 65–68  (so ML stops being magic)
             ↓
        REGRESSION → GRADIENT DESCENT   ← Days 71–73  (Chapter 1 calculus pays off here)
             ↓
        VALIDATION → CLASSIFICATION     ← Days 74–78  (the part most self-taught
             ↓                                          people get wrong)
        ▣ PROJECT 3  End-to-end ML      ← ships Day 85
             ↓
        PORTFOLIO → INTERVIEW READINESS ← Days 87–90
```

**Where this deliberately differs from the roadmaps in `Tem.txt`:**

| Change | Reason |
|:-------|:-------|
| **SQL moves from week 12–14 to Day 33**, and runs as a thin parallel track instead of a block | Entry-level hiring lists SQL ahead of machine learning. SQL has no prerequisites, so blocking it late wastes months. 25-minute query sessions are also the right work for a tired university evening. |
| **Statistics moves before machine learning, not after** | You cannot evaluate a model without understanding sampling, variance and significance. Every roadmap that teaches `.fit()` before p-values produces someone who cannot tell a good model from a lucky one. |
| **Linear algebra sits immediately before ML, not in a separate "maths month"** | Eigenvectors learned in October and used in December are eigenvectors forgotten. Days 65–68 exist so Days 71–86 make sense. |
| **Calculus starts in Month 1, in small pieces** | Because MAT 265 is happening *now*. This is the one topic where the university and the challenge can share the same study hour. |
| **Deep learning is removed entirely** | Prerequisite failure, and irrelevant to the roles you would realistically apply for first. Documented in [Resources & Decisions](./Resources_and_Decisions.md). |

## 🏗️ Phase Architecture

Months are **four-week blocks**, so no week is ever split across two folders. Month 3 carries the extra six days because that is where the final project and portfolio work need slack.

<div align="center">

| Month | Days | Weeks | Theme | You leave able to… |
|:-----:|:----:|:-----:|:------|:-------------------|
| **1** | 1–28 | 1–4 | **Python you can build with** | Write and structure Python, including OOP, without looking things up constantly |
| **2** | 29–56 | 5–8 | **From arrays to answers** | Clean a real dataset, explore it, visualise it, and reason about it statistically |
| **3** | 57–90 | 9–13 | **Models, business, evidence** | Frame a business question, build and validate a model, and show your work |

</div>

### Month 1 — Days 1–28
Python occupies Days 1–27; Day 28 is the month review. That is 27 calendar days, but **eight of them are 30–75 minute days** because of orientation week and the start of semester — so the effective Python load is roughly 22 full days, inside the 20–25 you asked for. The phase ends with a timed closed-book assessment and a small OOP system you built on your own LifeOS data.

### Month 2 — Days 29–56
NumPy (3 days) is deliberately short — it is a substrate, not a destination. Pandas gets six days plus continuous use. Visualisation is never a block; it is woven into every EDA day, because nobody learns Matplotlib by watching Matplotlib. Project 1 ships on Day 45. Statistics runs Days 46–56 straight out of *Essential Math for Data Science*, with StatQuest as the intuition layer.

### Month 3 — Days 57–90
Business framing, then Project 2 in SQL, then linear algebra, then machine learning taught narrowly: regression, gradient descent derived by hand, classification, and an unusually heavy emphasis on **validation, leakage and error analysis**. Project 3 ships Day 85. The last three days turn everything into evidence.

## ⏱️ Time Model

| Day type | Budget | Shape |
|:---------|:------:|:------|
| **Weekday** | 60–90 min | ~25 min input · ~40 min hands-on · ~10 min review |
| **Weekend** | ~3 h | ~45 min input · ~2 h build · ~15 min review |
| **Review day** (last day of each week) | 45–60 min | Recall test + catch-up |
| **Total** | **≈ 13.5 h/week · ≈ 150 h of scheduled work over 90 days** | |

Every day is split into two blocks:

- **🔴 MUST DO** — the irreducible core. Designed to fit in **45–60 minutes on a bad day.** If you only ever do this, the curriculum still works.
- **🟢 IF TIME** — depth, extra practice, the second explanation. Skipping it costs nothing structural.

> [!WARNING]
> The previous version of this challenge estimated **6–7 hours per day** and stalled at Week 4. That is the specific failure being corrected. If a day here takes more than its budget, the day is wrong — cut the IF TIME block and move on.

**The ratio that matters:** roughly one third input, two thirds doing. A 90-minute day is never 90 minutes of video. Where a video is revision-level, watch it at 1.5–2×.

## 📚 Resource Strategy

Every resource is classified. If a resource has no classification, it is not in the plan.

| Tag | Meaning |
|:----|:--------|
| **PRIMARY** | The main explanation for that concept. Watch or read it properly. |
| **REINFORCEMENT** | A second teacher on a hard concept, used on purpose. |
| **PRACTICE** | Where you actually do the work. |
| **REFERENCE** | Open only when stuck. Never watched end to end. |

**Rules for every project:**

1. It must answer a question a human would actually ask.
2. It must end with a written **recommendation and its limitations** — this is where business analytics lives in this plan.
3. Its README must let a stranger reproduce it.
4. **No Titanic, no Iris, no Boston Housing.** Those datasets signal a tutorial, not an analyst. They are allowed only as throwaway 20-minute mechanics drills, never as portfolio pieces.

## 🔁 The Review System

| Cadence | When | Length | Purpose |
|:--------|:-----|:------:|:--------|
| **Daily** | End of every day | 5–10 min | Four questions at the bottom of each `Day_XX.md`. Not a journal. |
| **Weekly** | Day 7 of each week | 45–60 min | `Week_XX_Review.md` — a real recall test plus catch-up time · **10 of them** |
| **Monthly** | Days 28, 56, 90 | 90 min | `Month_X_Review.md` — capability audit against evidence, then adjust the next month |

> Weeks 4, 8 and 13 end on month-review days, so the monthly review covers the week as well. Doing both would be an hour of duplicated questions.

The weekly and monthly reviews are **allowed to change the plan.** That is their job. Each one ends with an explicit decision: compress, expand, repeat, or continue.

- If you can already do something → **compress it** and take the days back.
- If an assessment goes badly → **schedule remediation** into the next week's IF TIME blocks.
- If a concept finally clicked after three days → those three days were correct.

## 📏 The Rules

1. **Watch less, build more.** If a day becomes video-only, it has failed. Target one third input, two thirds hands-on.
2. **Never watch a full course end to end.** Enter by timestamp, leave when the concept is yours.
3. **MUST DO is the contract. IF TIME is a bonus.** Never let IF TIME push you past the budget.
4. **Code from memory before checking.** Every assessment is closed-book by default.
5. **No new resource without a reason.** Every resource in this plan is tagged. A resource you found on YouTube today is not in the plan until you can say which tag it gets and what it replaces.
6. **Academics are the hard floor.** If a university deadline and this plan collide, the plan yields. Always.
7. **Commit daily.** You already have this habit — this repository should show it.
8. **Write the recommendation, not just the chart.** Every analysis ends in a sentence a decision-maker could act on.

## ✅ What "Done" Means

**A day is done** when its 📦 Completion Criteria block is satisfied — not when the clock runs out and not when the video ends.

**A week is done** when the review's recall test is completed *in writing, without notes*, and the next week is either confirmed or adjusted.

**The 90 days are done** when:

- [ ] Three portfolio repositories exist, each with a README a stranger can follow
- [ ] Essential Math Chapters 1–6 and 8 are complete, with the code you wrote for them
- [ ] You can write a multi-table SQL query with a window function without help
- [ ] You can explain, out loud and without notes: why n−1, what a p-value is not, what gradient descent does, and why your model's accuracy is misleading
- [ ] Day 90's assessment is written and honest
- [ ] The next 90 days are planned from evidence, not enthusiasm

## 🚑 How To Recover After Missed Days

This will happen. It is planned for.

| Situation | What to do |
|:----------|:-----------|
| **Missed one day** | Do that day's 🔴 MUST DO only, tomorrow, in addition. Skip its IF TIME. Do not "catch up" by doubling. |
| **Missed 2–3 days** | Do not replay them. Go to the next **review day** and use it entirely for catch-up — that is an explicitly correct use of a review day. Take the MUST DO blocks of the missed days only. |
| **Missed a week** | Do not shift Day numbers. Keep the calendar; drop the week's IF TIME entirely and merge its MUST DO into the following week's weekend. Note it in the weekly review. |
| **Exams / hackathon / illness** | Stop. Mark the days. Resume at the day you stopped on. The sequence matters; the calendar does not. |
| **Repeatedly behind by Day 28 or 56** | The monthly review has a reduction protocol. Use it. Cut scope — never raise hours. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The sequence is the plan. The dates are a scaffold.** Finishing Day 90 on 3 January instead of 14 December is a success. Skipping statistics to stay on schedule is not.
